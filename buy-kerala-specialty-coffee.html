Buyer’s guide · From interest to a useful enquiry

Buying Kerala specialty coffee.

Know the lot. Taste the coffee. Build a relationship with the people behind it.

A good coffee purchase begins before the price list. It begins with knowing what you need, who grew the coffee and what is actually available.

Perhaps you tasted a memorable Kerala coffee at a gathering. Perhaps you run a café, roast for other cafés, or want a new origin for your home brew. The next step is to connect that experience to a particular coffee, with enough information to make a confident choice.

This guide offers a practical route from curiosity to an enquiry. Kerala Origins can help start conversations with the community; current availability, samples, prices and delivery arrangements must be confirmed for each coffee.

Start with a short brief: your brewing method, the kind of cup you enjoy, your approximate quantity and where the coffee needs to go. A café buying an everyday espresso needs a different conversation from a roaster exploring a limited seasonal release.

Choose the right starting point

Green coffee or roasted beans?

  • RoastedFor home brewing or a café without a roastery. Ask for the roast date, recommended brewing use, pack size and the roaster’s advice on when to begin brewing. Choose whole beans if you can grind them; otherwise specify your brewer.
  • GreenFor a roaster or buyer working with a roasting partner. Ask for a sample linked to the sale lot, its harvest and processing records, available quantity, storage information and physical quality measurements where available.

Neither an origin name nor an elaborate process description tells you whether a coffee suits your needs. The Specialty Coffee Association connects specialty coffee with distinctive attributes and value; the useful question is which attributes matter to you and your customers.[1]

Ask about this harvest

Know the lot behind the name.

A farm can produce several coffees in one season. A lot identifies the particular batch being offered, rather than every bean from an estate. Request the producer or producer group, district, species or recorded variety, harvest period and processing method. Ask whether the sample represents the coffee you will receive.

Be open to Kerala’s range of coffees. Robusta is the familiar name for Coffea canephora, a species with substantial genetic diversity.[3] A species label is a starting point for discovery, not a tasting result. Our guide to Kerala’s coffee regions and producers gives that exploration a geographical context.

For experimental processing, ask for an understandable account of what happened: fermentation conditions, duration, drying method and whether anything was added. Read our guide to washed, natural and honey processing before comparing labels. Missing information is worth discussing openly rather than filling with assumptions.

Make tasting useful

Compare samples, then brew for your purpose.

Keep a record of the sample name, lot reference, date and preparation. When comparing coffees, use a consistent preparation and write your own observations before discussing them. Separate what you notice from what you prefer: a prominent fruit character may be interesting without being the right choice for your everyday espresso.

The SCA’s Coffee Value Assessment distinguishes physical attributes, sensory descriptions, impressions of quality and information about the coffee. That separation helps explain why a useful buying conversation needs more than a score.[2] If someone supplies a score, ask which assessment was used, who evaluated the sample and when.

A café should also trial the roasted coffee on its own equipment, in the drinks it intends to serve. A roaster can discuss sample-roast preparation before interpreting differences between green samples. Keep the feedback specific: what worked, what needs another trial and what information remains unclear.

From sample to order

Put the practical details in writing.

Once a coffee looks promising, confirm the exact lot, quantity, price basis, packaging, dispatch timing and destination. Clarify who invoices, arranges transport and handles a quality concern. For green coffee, agree how the delivered coffee will be compared with the approved sample and who keeps a reference sample.

Discuss repeat availability before building a menu around one lot. A seasonal coffee may finish, and the next harvest can differ. Our Kerala coffee harvest-season guide explains why harvest, processing and release dates belong in the same conversation.

For international enquiries, establish the seller, exporter and importer before discussing a shipment. The Coffee Board publishes exporter-registration, permit and certificate-of-origin information.[4] Use current official guidance and confirm destination requirements with the responsible parties. A Kerala Origins introduction does not itself confirm export readiness or delivery terms.

Start a conversation

Tell us what you are looking for.

You do not need a perfect sourcing brief. Share the details you already know, and identify the questions you would like help with. This simple structure makes the first conversation more useful:

“We are a [café / roastery / home brewer] based in [place]. We are looking for [green / roasted] Kerala coffee for [brewing use]. Our approximate requirement is [quantity or trial size]. We are interested in [region, process or cup preferences, if known]. Could we discuss available coffees, sample options and the information recorded for each lot?”

Explore the producer directory, then contact Kerala Origins. The aim is a relationship in which the producer receives clear credit, the buyer understands the coffee, and both sides know what happens next.
